The Tribe Needs Both

Imagine a tribe of 150 people—roughly Dunbar's number, the natural size of human social groups throughout evolution.

~90
Protectors
~60
Explorers

Protectors

Stay close to the center. Guard what works. They value:

  • Tradition and proven methods
  • Safety and stability
  • Clear boundaries and rules
  • Loyalty to the group
  • Caution toward outsiders

Explorers

Venture to the edges. Find new resources. They value:

  • Novelty and new experiences
  • Openness to change
  • Individual expression
  • Welcoming strangers
  • Questioning traditions
The evolutionary logic: A tribe of all protectors would never find new food sources or adapt to change. A tribe of all explorers would lose its culture and cohesion. The tension between them is the balance.
"The liberal and conservative impulses are both necessary for a functioning society—like the gas pedal and brake in a car."

Neither is right or wrong. They're different survival strategies that evolved because groups needed both.

Note: Real political views are more complex than this simple model. This is about psychological tendencies, not policies.
