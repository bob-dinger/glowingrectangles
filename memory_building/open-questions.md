# Open questions

Decisions not yet made. Each has a recommendation, but none are settled.

---

## 1. What are the ten stations?

The biggest open item. Needs a set of ten place-types that are:

- **visually unmistakable from each other**, including in silhouette at distance
- **buildable in every biome** — a well works in a desert and a tundra; a pier
  does not work away from water
- **orderable into a natural walk**, so the sequence feels like a route rather
  than a checklist

Straw man to argue with:

| # | Station | Reads as |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The gate | You have arrived |
| 2 | The great tree | Single tall silhouette |
| 3 | The well | Low, circular, look down |
| 4 | The cabin | Enterable, has an interior |
| 5 | The bridge | You cross something |
| 6 | The tower | Tall, you look out from it |
| 7 | The barn | Big volume, wide door |
| 8 | The pond | Flat, reflective, low |
| 9 | The shrine | Small, ornate, stop and look |
| 10 | The overlook | High point, you see the next region |

Note the halfway marker falls naturally: **the bridge is 5**, so crossing means
you are halfway through the ten. And **the overlook is 10**, which points at
where you are going next — the route explains itself.

Open sub-question: should the peg object sit *at* the station, or *inside* it?
Inside is stronger (the cabin door becomes a reveal) but costs interiors for
every station in every region.

## 2. Regions: eras or arbitrary buckets?

Currently arbitrary — region N holds stops 10N-9 to 10N.

**For eras** (founding / expansion / civil war / industrial / modern): the
coarse index would carry historical meaning, so the biome tells you something
true rather than just which bucket.

**Against:** eras are not ten presidents long. You would lose the clean
10/10/10/10/7 split, and uneven regions weaken the "which ten" arithmetic that
is the entire reason for chunking.

**Recommendation:** keep the arbitrary tens. The chunking is doing memory work;
era-mapping would be doing *thematic* work, and it costs the thing that makes
the structure function. Reconsider only if the tens turn out to be easy anyway.

## 3. Do the five biomes stay as chosen?

Currently Alpine / Arcology / Winter / Sand Sea / Boreal Forest, with seven more
as unnumbered wilderness between them.

Worth checking: Alpine and Winter are both cold and pale, and both were picked
partly because they look good. They are the likeliest pair to blur into each
other. Consider swapping Winter for something chromatically further away —
Red Mesa and Volcanic Waste are both already built and both are unmistakable.

## 4. Are the pegs actually yours?

All 45 were picked by me. Mnemonics are personal — a peg that does not stick is
worse than useless, because it costs rehearsal time and returns nothing.

Walk the palace once and mark the dead ones. Rework those specifically rather
than reviewing all 45.

Known weak candidates, flagged honestly: **19 Hayes → haze** (abstract, nothing
to look at), **18 Grant → oversized cheque** (thin pun, easily confused with
other paper), **33 Truman → painted-sky dome** (too conceptual to picture fast).

## 5. Does the corridor palace get retired?

Assumed yes, once the landscape carries the pegs. But the corridor has one real
advantage: enclosure. A room with four walls forces attention onto one object;
open landscape does not.

If landscape recall turns out weaker in practice, the answer may be a hybrid —
the landscape as the map and index, with each *station* being an enclosed
interior you step into. That is more work but keeps both properties.

Decide by testing, not in advance.

## 6. Rehearsal, not just authoring

Everything built so far helps you *make* the palace. Nothing yet helps you
*rehearse* it on a schedule.

Quiz mode exists in the corridor palace but is passive. Missing: which pegs you
actually failed, and when to see them again. A spaced-repetition layer over the
47 stops is probably worth more than any further visual polish.
