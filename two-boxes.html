Two Boxes

The same eight notes, fingered two different ways. The octave run keeps your hand as small as possible. The playing position keeps three notes on every string so it can carry on across all six. They are not the same shape, and for four of the seven roots they don't even use the same frets.

Why they differ

There are only three ways to finger three consecutive scale notes on one string, and which one you get depends only on the degree you start from:

A whole, whole  ·  frets f, f+2, f+4  ·  span 4, the stretch
B whole, half  ·  f, f+2, f+3  ·  span 3
C half, whole  ·  f, f+1, f+3  ·  span 3

Across degrees 1 to 7 that reads A B C A A B C. The position is just that row read in steps of three, since each string starts a third above the one below it.

The run refuses the A shape when it can. It will drop back to a lower fret on the next string up rather than stretch — which is why it stays inside four frets, and why it sometimes moves backwards across the neck. The position accepts every stretch the scale hands it, and gets uniformity and reach in exchange.

Neither is wrong. The run is the cheaper hand for one octave. The position is the shape that keeps going. The A shape lands on degrees 1, 4 and 5 — exactly the major chords — because two whole steps above a note is a major third.
