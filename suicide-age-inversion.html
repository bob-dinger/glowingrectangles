The age gradient inverted

US suicide rate by age, 1932 against 2023. In the Depression, suicide climbed steeply with age — the old killed themselves seven times as often as the young. Today the line is nearly flat.

+86%7.3 → 13.5
Ages 65–74

Ages 45–64
−57%~44 → 18.8

Every age over 45 improved enormously. The young got worse. The two curves cross at about age 40.

Male ÷ female, all ages
3.86× 1932

Ninety-one years, a rebuilt economy, and the sex ratio did not move at all.

Both series are crude rates, so they are directly comparable. The headline number you may have seen — 17.4 in 1932 against 14.2 today — is not a valid comparison: the modern all-ages series is age-adjusted and the 1932 one is not.

1932 rates are published by age. 2023 rates are published by sex within age; the both-sexes figure here is deaths ÷ implied population, from CDC counts. Bins differ between eras, so each series is drawn as steps across the ages it actually covers.

Source: CDC/NCHS vital statistics.
