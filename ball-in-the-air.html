A Ball In The Air

Throw a ball and it does not travel in a straight line. Everyone knows that. The correction they teach you is a parabola — and that is not right either.
A parabola assumes gravity pulls the same direction everywhere, which is another way of saying it assumes the ground is flat. The real path is an ellipse with the centre of the Earth at one focus. Every throw is a piece of an orbit that happens to hit the ground first.
how hard you throw it
straight — no gravity at all
parabola — flat ground, gravity straight down
ellipse — round Earth, gravity toward a point
Both paths solved exactly, not simulated. The flat range is v·√(2h/g). The true path is the conic through the same initial state: p = h²/GM with h = r₀v, e = |p/r₀ − 1|, and r(θ) = p/(1 − e cos θ). Ground distance is the arc R·Δθ. GM = 3.986×10¹⁴ m³/s², R = 6,371 km, g = 9.80665. Air resistance ignored throughout — which for a real bullet matters far more than the curvature does.
