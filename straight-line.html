There Are No Straight Lines

A textbook prints a quantity as a line and hands you the whole of it at once, seen from outside, already finished. Nobody has ever experienced a quantity that way.
And over a short enough stretch the line is right. Population growth is a ruler for five years. Over a hundred it is not. Shrink the window below and watch a straight line become the correct answer — that is not a failure of reasoning, it is the reasoning working on the evidence available.
You live inside the thing while it is changing. So each figure is printed twice: once the way a book would set it, and once from the inside of a world that grows and shrinks year by year around whoever is standing in it.
after Hans Rosling, Factfulness — the straight line instinct
