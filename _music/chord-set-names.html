Every Chord Set

Six chords live in a major key once you drop the diminished seventh, so there are exactly 20 sets of three and 15 sets of four — and that is all of them. Each is named for the most famous song whose section uses exactly that palette, and the number is how many songs in the corpus do. Order is not part of the identity: C G Am F and C F G Am are the same set.

Watch the red slot. Sets that hold the tonic average 104 songs; sets without it average 24 — four times fewer, and it holds separately for the threes and the fours. Almost the whole ranking is “does it contain home”.

The exception is worth knowing: (Don’t Fear) The Reaper, F G Am, has no C in it at all and still lands 105 songs — ahead of most sets that do. Its trick is that Am can stand in for home.

Only four sets contain all three pillars, red green blue: Pillars, Axis, Wouldn’t It Be Nice and I Feel Fine. They rank 1st, 1st, 2nd and 4th.

Dm Em Am is exactly the three minor chords — and it is the rarest set of three, at six songs. Add one chord to it and you get three of the fours: add F for September, add G for And I Love Her, add C for Shake Me Down.

C Em G is the tonic triad with each note harmonised — a C chord's own notes, turned back into chords.
