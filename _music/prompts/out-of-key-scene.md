# One image for the eighteen out-of-key pairs

The three chords from outside C major, each paired with one of the six inside it.
**A♯ = p/b, D = th/w, E = r** — the exact sounds the diatonic scenes ban, which is why
those scenes never use them and this one is built from nothing else.

Three out-of-key chords × six in-key = **18 pairs**, and every one spells a short word.

**Six things HAPPENING, three items each** — a night market on the edge of town, which is
where out-of-key chords live: recognisable, adjacent, not quite home.

## The prompt

> A wide, richly detailed storybook illustration of a roadside night market on the edge of
> town, lit by strung bulbs and a single street lamp, painted in warm gouache with clear
> readable staging. Six separate pieces of action, none overlapping.
>
> **Far left — the wolf, the ram, the swan:** a grey **wolf** lunges at a curl-horned
> **ram**, and a white **swan** rears up with wings spread, driving the wolf back.
>
> **Left — the czar, the wig, the cash:** a portly **czar** in a crooked powdered **wig**
> and a fur coat counts a thick **wad of banknotes** into a trader's outstretched hand.
>
> **Centre — the referee, the punch, the bat:** a striped **referee** flings out an arm to
> block a man mid-**punch**, while a third man behind them holds a wooden **bat** raised.
>
> **Right — the bus, the map, the car:** a painted **bus** and a small **car** have stopped
> nose to nose, their drivers arguing over a huge paper **map** spread across both bonnets.
>
> **Foreground right — the rat, the beef, the jar:** a fat **rat** drags a slab of raw
> **beef** under a stall table, knocking over a glass **jar** that rolls after it.
>
> **Above, around the lamp — the moth, the bug, the washing:** a big pale **moth** and a
> green **beetle** circle the street lamp, throwing shadows across a line of **washing**
> strung between two stalls.
>
> Storybook illustration, warm night palette, lamplight and bulbs, everything clearly legible.

## Why these three chords, and why this is a separate set

Measured over 4,254 corpus sections, they do not behave alike:

| slot | diatonic | out-of-key | both in one section |
|---|---|---|---|
| D | ii (1065) | II / V/V (352) | 64 — 18% |
| E | iii (741) | III / V/vi (204) | 24 — 12% |
| A | vi (1858) | ♭VII (781) | **374 — 48%** |

D and E really are near-substitutes: the major version appears *instead of* the minor, and
when both turn up it is because they are doing different jobs — II/V/V resolves to V 26% of
the time, III/V/vi resolves to vi 35% of the time. They are secondary dominants, pulling
somewhere; the minors are not.

**A♯ is not a substitute for anything.** ♭VII goes to I 41% of the time — a plagal step down,
not a dominant — and it sits beside vi in half the sections that have both. I–♭VII–vi is a
descent, not a contradiction. So ♭VII deserves to be counted as a seventh chord rather than
folded into the A slot.
