Ten Thousand A Week

Two worlds, drawn at the same scale. Every panel on the surface is a thousand Segways — so a bigger world is not a bigger drawing, it is more panels, and you can count them.
Because the panels are a fixed size, the surface area is the count. Radius follows as the square root of it — nothing here is scaled for effect.

spent developing the Segway PT before launch. The money moved first, while demand was still exactly zero.
The belief was not stupid. It was induction — a very big conclusion from very little data, the same machinery that gets language and cause right. It simply ran ahead of the one measurement that settles a product, which always arrives last.
Cumulative units from the Segway Inc. record: 6,000 by 2003; ~23,500 by September 2006, every one of which was recalled that month; 30,000 by 2007; over 50,000 by March 2009; roughly 140,000 across the product's life. Launched December 2001 at about $5,000; production ended 15 July 2020. Ten thousand a week is Dean Kamen's figure, widely reported rather than filed. Only the milestones are known; between them the count is drawn straight.
