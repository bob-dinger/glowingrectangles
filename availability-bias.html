What is Availability Bias?

We judge how common something is by how easily examples come to mind.

Things that are vivid, recent, or emotional are easier to recall—so we think they happen more often than they do.

"If you can think of it, it must be important."

In This Demo

The observer only notices the gold circles because they stand out. Blue circles pass by unnoticed.

Even though blue vastly outnumbers gold, the observer would guess gold is more common.

Real World Examples

Shark Attacks vs. Vending Machines
Shark attacks get news coverage. Vending machine deaths don't. We fear sharks more, though vending machines kill more people.
Plane Crashes vs. Car Accidents
Plane crashes are memorable. We forget the 100+ daily car deaths. Flying feels scarier than driving.
Lottery Winners
We see winners on TV. We don't see the millions who lost. Winning feels more likely than it is.

Studied by Tversky & Kahneman (1973)
