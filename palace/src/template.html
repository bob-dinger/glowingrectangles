The Presidential Memory Palace

Every president is one absurd object in one room, and the rooms run in a single unbranching line. Walk it start to finish and the order comes with you — that's the whole trick. A palace works because your memory for places you have been is far better than your memory for lists.

Two pegging systems are mixed. Some are sound-alikes — Buchanan is a cannon, Coolidge a cool fridge, Biden a bidet. Others are the image the man already owns: Lincoln's top hat, Nixon's tape recorder. Related presidents share an escalating image, so the pair is stored as a pair — Adams is an atom and his son is an atom bomb; Bush is a shrub and his son is a burning one.

Grover Cleveland and Donald Trump each served two non-consecutive terms, so each has one room you enter twice. The president who interrupted them waits in a dead-end alcove off that same room. You go in, get interrupted, and come back out — the building teaches the fact.

Every room is papered differently too — a second cue for where you are, independent of the object on the plinth.

You start above it, looking down at the whole route. Click any room to drop into it, or walk from the beginning. Once inside: W A S D to move, drag or the arrow keys to look around, shift to run. O returns you to the overhead view, M opens the index, and Q is quiz mode, which hides the names and leaves you alone with the room.
