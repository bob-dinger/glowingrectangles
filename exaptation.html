What is Exaptation?

Exaptation is when a trait that evolved for one purpose gets co-opted for a completely different use.

Unlike adaptation (where a feature evolves specifically for its current use), exaptation shows evolution's creative recycling.

"Evolution doesn't design from scratch. It tinkers with what's already there."

The Bird Wing Story

Stage 1: Early dinosaurs developed proto-feathers for temperature regulation. They helped keep warm or cool down.

Stage 2: Some realized these feathery limbs helped them glide when jumping between branches or running from predators.

Stage 3: Over millions of years, these same structures became optimized for powered flight.

Other Examples

Feathers: Insulation → Display → Flight
Originally for warmth, then used to attract mates, finally for flying.
Swim bladders → Lungs
Fish bladders for buoyancy became lungs for breathing air.
Jaw bones → Middle ear
Reptile jaw bones became the tiny bones that let mammals hear.

Term coined by Stephen Jay Gould and Elisabeth Vrba (1982)
