Octaves

Every place one note lives on the neck, and the shapes that connect them. The fretboard spans 3.4 octaves — low E open is E2, the high E string at fret 17 is A5.

Note

Why there are only four

An octave is twelve semitones. Every string pair is a perfect fourth — five semitones — except G to B, which is a major third. So jumping two strings normally covers ten semitones, leaving two frets to make up. Cross the G–B seam and it only covers nine, so you need three.

Same story going three strings: normally fifteen semitones, so you come back three frets. Cross the seam and it's fourteen, so you only come back two.

Two of the four shapes are the plain ones and two are the seam. The seam is the reason guitar has any irregularity at all — and the reason these have to be memorised in pairs rather than learned once.
